What Really Shaped Me

Alumni magazine · column: Formative Moments

Every month, the university alumni magazine asks a former student what made them who they are. This month it's Dr Sofía Ramírez, an astrophysicist who grew up in a small town in northern Chile. You are helping the editor prepare her interview, her research summary and her column. Everything in this homework comes from Sofía's pages.

Part 1 · Add the emphasis

The editor wants Sofía's answers to sound stronger. Rewrite each sentence with the frame given.

1My grandfather showed me the stars. It was…

2I fell in love with physics in the Atacama Desert. It was in…

It was in the Atacama Desert that I fell in love with physics.

3I needed a good telescope. What…

What I needed was a good telescope.

4The questions attracted me to astronomy, not the answers. It wasn't… It was…

It wasn't the answers that attracted me to astronomy. It was the questions.

5I only wanted to understand how stars are born. All…

All I wanted was to understand how stars are born.

6I studied in Germany because the best astronomy courses were there. The reason…

The reason I studied in Germany was that the best astronomy courses were there.

7I didn't start observing real stars until 2015. It was not until…

It was not until 2015 that I started observing real stars.

8My first supervisor taught me to accept mistakes. What my first supervisor…

What my first supervisor taught me was to accept mistakes.

Tip of the week

Never drop the "It"

Portuguese uses the same structure as English It-clefts all the time, so you already know how to think in clefts. But Portuguese starts the sentence with the verb, and English can't. The two most common mistakes for Brazilian speakers:

✗ Was my grandfather who…✓ It was my grandfather who taught me.
✗ What I need it is…✓ What I need is more time. (no extra it)

One more: for times and places, formal English prefers that, not when or where: It was in 2015 that I started. Practise it this week: every time you emphasise something in Portuguese, say the English version in your head.
