Grace's Scholarship Interview

The situation

Grace Mwangi is from Kenya. She has applied for a master's scholarship in public health, and her interview is next week. You are going to help her prepare: check her interview questions, her personal statement and her mentor's feedback. Then it's your turn to answer the interview questions.

Grace and the people in her story are fictional.

Part 1 · Questions (Lesson 1)

Grace wrote down questions for the interview. Each one has a mistake. Correct it.

Quick reminder

Word order: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb: Where did you grow up?
Subject questions: no do / did: Who told you?
Prepositions stay at the end: What does it focus on?
Polite questions use statement word order: Could you tell me where the office is?

1Where you did your undergraduate degree?

Where did you do your undergraduate degree?

2Who did inspire you to apply? (Grace wants to know the person.)

Who inspired you to apply?

3What does your research focus?

What does your research focus on?

4Could you tell me when is the deadline?

Could you tell me when the deadline is?

5How long you have been interested in public health?

How long have you been interested in public health?

6Do you can tell me more about the funding?

Could you tell me more about the funding?

Part 2 · Past simple or present perfect? (Lesson 2)

The first paragraph of Grace's personal statement. Choose the right form.

Quick reminder

Past simple: finished time or one specific study: in 2016, two years ago, after I graduated · Otieno (2021) found…
Present perfect: up to now or the research field in general: since then, to date, ever · Research has shown…

Part 3 · Make it stronger (Lesson 3)

Grace wants to emphasise the key ideas in her statement. Rewrite each sentence with a cleft, starting with the words given.

Quick reminder

It was my teacher who changed my mind. (never drop It)
What I need is more time. (no extra it)
It was not until 2015 that I understood.
It is X, rather than Y, that… (to correct a belief)

1My grandmother inspired me to work in health. → It was…

It was my grandmother who inspired me to work in health.

2I need more research experience. → What…

What I need is more research experience.

3I didn't understand the value of community work until 2020. → It was not until…

It was not until 2020 that I understood the value of community work.

4Trust, not money, makes families accept vaccines. → It is trust, rather than…

It is trust, rather than money, that makes families accept vaccines.

Part 5 · Speak

Now it's your interview.
